# November 13, 2026

## What We Built

- Every SaaSassin product is now legally open source under AGPL-3.0, with contributing
  and private security-reporting guides. The repository had been public without a
  license, which meant nobody could actually use it.
- A daily build-and-market loop now runs at 11:00: it picks the biggest gap against
  the focus competitor, builds and ships it when every check passes, and drafts the
  story (changelog blurb, social posts, short clip script). Its runbook lives in
  `docs/loop/README.md`; focus and history in `docs/loop/`.
- Lattice is the first competitor. Its research, a 119-row parity map (30 have,
  18 partial, 71 missing), the next 15 one-day slices, and the positioning story with
  a video script and compare-page brief are in `docs/competitors/lattice/`.
- **Per-product open-source repositories.** Any of the ten products can now be exported as
  its own standalone, self-hostable Next.js repository (`saasassin-<product>`) with
  `scripts/export-product.mjs`. Each one installs, builds, lints, typechecks and passes its
  own unit, rules and emulator tests on its own, serves the product at `/`, and ships a
  README with a self-host quickstart, a "Deploy with Vercel" button, its Firestore rules
  and an `.env.example` limited to what it reads.
- **Publishing.** `scripts/publish-products.mjs` syncs those repositories from the monorepo:
  a dry run by default, `--commit` / `--push` to commit `sync: saasassin@<sha>` only when
  something changed. It never force-pushes.
- **Standalone routing.** With `STANDALONE_PRODUCT=<slug>` every host serves only that
  product at the root, and the shared fleet session/tenant APIs refuse other products.
  Nothing changes when it is unset.

- **All ten product repositories are live on GitHub** — `alibad/saasassin-performance`,
  `-research`, `-planner`, `-creator`, `-feedback`, `-studies`, `-revenue`, `-community`,
  `-mail` and `-trust` — public, AGPL-3.0, with Discussions on for questions, topics for
  search (`<competitor>-alternative`, `open-source`, `self-hosted`, `nextjs`, `firebase`),
  a homepage pointing at each live product, and a README with a Deploy-with-Vercel button.

- **saasassin.net/compare/lattice** — an indexable, sourced comparison of SaaSassin
  Performance and Lattice: every Lattice fact carries a dated source, a cost calculator
  shows Lattice's list price (including its $4,000 annual minimum) against $0, a live
  parity progress section updates from the parity map on every deploy, and a "Talk to
  us" form sits on the page. Adding the next competitor is one data file.
- **saasassin.net/contact** — anyone can reach the team from the footer of every page,
  including product subdomains.
- Performance can now be demoed and recorded end to end without touching production:
  `scripts/seed-demo-performance.mjs` fills local Auth and Firestore emulators with a
  fictional 30-person company (Northwind Robotics) — directory, two templates, a fall
  cycle taken through nominations, writing, calibration and released packets, a
  quarterly check-in half written, goals with check-ins, feedback, one-to-ones, a
  saved view, an API key, a webhook and two months of audit history. It runs the real
  data layer, so every record and audit event is exactly what the product writes.
- The first story video for SaaSassin Performance vs Lattice (16:9, about three
  minutes) plus a 9:16 short, thumbnail and captions were recorded from that demo
  workspace; the upload kit is a draft in `marketing/drafts/` (nothing published).

## What We Fixed

- **Privacy:** every Performance member's browser received the organization's full audit
  history (up to 100 events, e.g. "Gave constructive feedback to …"), and Overview showed
  it as "Recent activity" — only the Audit tab was hidden. The server now sends the full
  history only to roles with `audit.read`; everyone else gets just their own actions.
  An emulator test locks this in.

- SaaSassin Performance no longer calls itself "Lattice Performance" in page titles
  and on its sign-in page, removing a trademark risk ahead of comparison marketing.
- Product pages linked to `/targets/...` on their own host, which breaks once a product
  lives outside the monorepo. Shells and sign-in pages now link to the public briefs on
  `https://saasassin.net` absolutely.
- Target pages gain an `#interest` anchor so product READMEs link straight to the join form.

## Key Decisions

- AGPL-3.0 for every product: free to use and self-host; hosted modified versions must
  share their changes. Compatible with Fider for the Feedback upstream path.
- The loop ships automatically only when lint, typecheck, unit, rules, emulator and
  build checks all pass; otherwise it parks work on a `loop/<date>` branch.
- Marketing stays draft-only: the owner decides every upload and post.
- Each product will also be published as its own repository, generated from this
  monorepo, which remains the single source of truth. Product repositories are never
  edited by hand; accepted changes land here first.
- **Trace, don't list.** The exporter follows the import graph from each product's routes,
  root layout and proxy, so new files ship automatically and parent-site code (catalog,
  roadmap, ops, Logo Lab) or another product can never leak in; the export fails if it would.
- **Same dependency versions everywhere,** so the copied `pnpm-lock.yaml` installs with
  `--frozen-lockfile`.
- `STANDALONE_PRODUCT` is inlined through `next.config.ts` `env`, so a fresh Vercel deploy
  of a product repository works without configuring it.
  monorepo, which remains the single source of truth.
- Emulator mode is opt-in and fenced: the browser and Admin SDK switch to the
  emulators only outside production builds, only with explicit emulator hosts, and
  only for a `demo-` project id. A half-set environment (one host, or a real project
  id) throws instead of mixing emulator and production traffic. With the variables
  unset nothing changes. The seed refuses to run under the same conditions and drops
  any real credentials from its environment first.
- Demo data uses reserved `.example` email addresses and endpoints, so nothing in a
  recording can reach a real inbox or server; the demo webhook fails on purpose and
  the delivery log says so.

## Breaking Changes

- None. Monorepo routing and pages behave as before; only the target-brief links now open
  `saasassin.net` instead of the current host.

## Product brands

### What We Built

- **Ten products, ten identities.** Performance, Research, Planner, Creator, Feedback, Studies,
  Revenue, Community, Mail and Trust each have their own palette, typefaces, corner radius,
  abstract mark, favicon, home-screen icon, social card and light/dark themes (Mail defaults to
  dark). Nothing inside a product looks like SaaSassin any more; SaaSassin appears only as the
  footer line "Part of the SaaSassin open-source family" and the source link.
- **A real homepage for every product.** Signed-out visitors now see a product site instead of the
  internal incubation dossier: what it does, a preview of the main screen, features, how it
  works, importing from the tool they use today (only where the importer exists: Dovetail,
  Kajabi, Canny, Maze, Circle — plus .ics calendars for Planner, .mbox/.eml for Mail and
  transcripts for Revenue), open source and data ownership, honest FAQs that name what isn't
  built yet, and a sign-up call to action. Layouts differ per product (Research editorial,
  Mail dark and keyboard-led, Planner calendar-led, Community warm and social, Trust precise
  with a "What Trust is not" block, Revenue data-led with metric definitions).
- Performance gets a homepage too: performance.saasassin.net no longer jumps straight to sign-in.
- **Branded sign-in pages** with a product panel beside the form for all ten products; the
  Performance form drops the SaaSassin lockup and its brutalist styling.
- Visitors can switch each product between light, dark and their system setting from the footer,
  and the choice is remembered without a flash on reload. Dialogs and menus follow the brand.
- Titles read "Research — The customer research repository" and "Sign in — Research"; product
  homepages are indexable and listed in the sitemap, while sign-in and workspace pages stay out
  of search results as before.

### What We Fixed

- Product pages showed the SaaSassin favicon next to their own icon; the site favicon now lives
  in `public/` so only saasassin.net uses it.
- Buttons rendered as links inside products took their parent's text colour (dark text on dark
  buttons); product links now follow the button styles.

### Key Decisions

- **Brand = tokens + fonts + mark, applied at the product's layout.** Each product layout wraps
  its routes in `BrandRoot` (`data-brand`, `data-mode`, the product's `next/font` variables) and
  imports only its own `src/brands/<slug>/theme.css`, so a product never downloads another
  product's fonts and a standalone export carries only its own brand. shadcn components keep
  working unchanged; the brand changes how they look.
- Every text colour pair in every brand is WCAG AA in light and dark, enforced by a test.
- Social cards and home-screen icons are static PNGs generated from the brand data and mark
  (`scripts/generate-brand-images.mjs`), not request-time image routes, so they can't fail in
  production.
- Product previews are built from the same UI components as the products, with fictional sample
  data, and switch to a real screenshot automatically once `public/products/<slug>/hero.webp`
  exists. No logos, testimonials or user counts.
- Product source links point at each product's own repository (`alibad/saasassin-<slug>`);
  Performance keeps the monorepo link the Lattice comparison already uses.

### Breaking Changes

- `FleetAppShell` (the incubation dossier) is deleted; that material lives on saasassin.net.
- Signed-out visits to `/lattice` (performance.saasassin.net) render the Performance homepage
  instead of redirecting to sign-in.
